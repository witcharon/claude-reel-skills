# Case studies: the Lidlezz reels

Lidlezz is a macOS app that keeps a Mac awake with the lid closed while Claude Code works, and lets it sleep when the work is done. Its verb: **close the lid, it keeps working.** These are calibration notes, not templates. Several of their devices are now spent.

## Flopped: "skydive v1", a fully CG reel built from one still
- A 3D freefall and a parallax photo with six stacked captions, a HUD, a chip and a notification.
- The user's verdict: "çok kötü olmuş, hiç istediğim gibi değil" ("it's really bad, nothing like what I wanted").
- **Lessons:** CG looked like a game; too many elements; no real action; the product never drove the story.

## Worked: skydive (Seedance take)
- A single continuous take: typing at the plane door, the lid slams, the jump, the canopy.
- Signature: the logo behind the canopy, a real Claude Code UI tracked onto the screen, and a 3D pull-back revealing the whole take was on a laptop before the lid shuts.
- **Lessons:** real footage and motion beat CG; the logo must stay readable when risers cross it; text must be centred and inside the safe zones.

## Worked well: Everest
- One prompt with three shots (tent, night climb, summit sunrise). The lid closes into total darkness where "still running." glows; a HUD altimeter and agent timer tick through; the logo rises from behind the horizon like the sun.
- The user's verdict: "harika olmuş… akıcılık, aradaki kesiş fikir çok güzel oturmuş" ("it's great… the flow, and the idea of the cuts in between worked really well").
- **Lessons:** the cut in darkness became a story beat, not just a seam; realistic terminal text size (oversized UI text looked fake); the Apple logo is fine.

## Worked: Mac POV ("pov: you're the mac.")
- The camera is the MacBook: webcam at a café, then inside a backpack. The zipper opens onto a roller coaster, a beach, a festival and a plane door. The HUD logs dry jokes and the clock fast-forwards through the dark. The Mac sleeps with a tape-stop and a CRT switch-off, wakes at home to a notification, and ends on a Nolan card with a "check the caption" CTA.
- **Lessons:** perspective sound (muffled vs. open) sells the conceit; the joke lives in specific log lines; text must respect the safe zones and be centred on 540; the end card should be long (~6 s) and point to the caption.

## Other concepts in the series
- **Orbit (space):** zero-g type, a logo drawn by city lights on the night side, a Nolan ending. The user asked for no punch-in zoom and for typing synced to the fingers.
- **Safari:** dramatic irony (the lion is seen first) and whisper-to-scream type on the roar.
- **F1 grid:** start lights as a built-in countdown, each labelled with a product state; a results-tower ending.

## Devices now spent (rest them)
Cut hidden in darkness, logo behind the subject, nature-made logo, Nolan card, extreme-location + laptop. Use them again only with a genuinely new twist, or when the user asks for a sequel.
