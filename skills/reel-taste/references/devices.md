# The spice rack

A concept is one choice from each shelf: **genre, hook, structure, motion signature, sound idea, ending**. Combine them freely, but keep one signature move per video, and change at least three shelves from the previous video.

Each entry says what it is, when it shines and when it turns into a cliché. Examples marked (L) are from the Lidlezz reels.

## 1. Genre wheel

Spin it on purpose; don't let every idea land in "extreme sport".

| Genre | Feel | Works for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme / adventure | awe, stakes | "works anywhere, unattended" | done three times for Lidlezz already (L); needs a new angle |
| Relatable comedy | recognition | daily pain the product removes | can be flat; needs one absurd escalation |
| POV inversion | cleverness | products that are objects or machines | the camera logic must stay consistent (L: Mac POV) |
| Mockumentary / nature doc | deadpan | turning the user into a species | the lower-third joke must be sharp |
| Sports broadcast | hype with structure | speed, competition, results | no real leagues, liveries or brands |
| Heist / thriller | tension, silence | security, stealth, "invisible" features | needs a clear payoff, not just mood |
| Found footage / CCTV / dashcam | realism, cheap to generate | "what happens when you're not looking" | picture must still be legible |
| Pastiche (90s infomercial, VHS, silent film, anime OP) | nostalgia, novelty | simple benefits, parody | the pastiche must fit the product's tone |
| Satisfying / ASMR | calm, loopable | smooth or automatic features | little story; needs one twist |
| Musical / rhythm edit | energy | anything with repetitive actions | cut to a real beat, not a stock one |
| Loop | rewatch value | cycles (daily, overnight, repeat) | the first and last frames must truly match |
| Split / comparison | clarity | before/after, with/without | easily becomes a boring ad; make one side absurd |
| Sci-fi / space | scale | "it runs while you're away" | L: orbit already used |

## 2. Hook devices (the first second)

- **Impossible juxtaposition.** An everyday object where it can't be: an open laptop in a plane door, a tent at 8,000 m. *Shines:* products used "anywhere". *Cliché when:* the place is just a pretty backdrop with nothing at stake.
- **Dramatic irony.** The viewer sees the threat before the character does, like a lion lying in the grass behind someone typing (L). *Shines:* tension and comedy. *Needs:* a threat that is clearly visible in a vertical frame.
- **POV inversion.** "pov: you're the mac.": the camera is the product (L). *Shines:* object products, machines, apps. *Needs:* the camera logic to stay consistent all the way through.
- **A countdown the world already has.** F1 start lights, a roller-coaster lift chain, a microwave, a pedestrian signal, a rocket T-minus (L: F1). *Shines:* building tension without pasted numbers.
- **A question in the image.** Why is there a laptop on the steering wheel? The viewer stays to find out.
- **Cold-open line.** One typed line on a screen that is the joke, e.g. `> keep going. i'm jumping.` (L). *Needs:* the screen to be trackable (see motion-handoff).
- **Wrong scale.** A whisper-size caption where a scream is expected, or a huge "why." at the most dramatic moment (L).

## 3. Structure devices

- **One continuous take.** Most immersive and most consistent from the generator. *Use* when the action fits one place.
- **Cuts hidden in darkness or a flash.** A lid closing, a zipper, a tunnel, a blink or a flash all hide seams and make multi-location stories possible (L: Everest, Mac POV). *Cliché when* used every time; it is currently overused.
- **Match cut on the product gesture.** Closing a lid → closing a car door → closing a book, one gesture carrying three worlds. *Needs* similar framing in each shot.
- **Escalation ladder.** Each scene more absurd than the last: roller coaster → beach → festival → plane door (L). Order by rising absurdity and end on the calmest beat.
- **Time-lapse via UI.** A clock or date in a HUD fast-forwards through the dark, telling hours or days without showing them (L: SAT 09:12 → SUN 21:47).
- **Reveal / pull-back.** "It was all on a screen" or "it was a toy". Once per brand, maximum (L: skydive 3D lid-close reveal).
- **Callback.** A shot referencing a previous video, e.g. the Mac POV bag at the plane door from the first skydive reel (L). Rewards followers.
- **Fake-out.** The obvious ending is set up, then something smaller and funnier lands.
- **Loop.** The last frame hands back to the first; good for rewatch metrics.

## 4. Motion signatures (pick one per video)

- **Real UI on the device.** The product's actual interface tracked onto a screen at realistic size (L: every reel). This is the baseline, not the signature.
- **Text behind the subject.** A logo or word between sky and foreground: behind a canopy, the horizon or a car's halo (L). *Needs:* a clean, separable background.
- **Whisper → scream type.** Tiny captions during tension, a screen-filling word on the release (L: safari).
- **World-locked labels.** Tags stuck to things in the scene, like each F1 start light labelled with a product state (L).
- **Diegetic HUD.** The product's own "vision": status, clock, log lines with dry jokes (L: Mac POV). *Needs:* a consistent machine voice.
- **Counters that tick with the world.** An altimeter, a speedometer, a progress bar tied to the action (L: Everest).
- **Nature-made logo.** The logo formed by the environment: rising like the sun, drawn by city lights, printed as a contrail. Strong ending, used twice already (L).
- **System-state moments.** A CRT switch-off when something sleeps, a boot-up when it wakes (L).
- **Notification as payoff.** The product's real notification lands exactly when the story resolves (L). Only if the feature exists.
- **Documentary / broadcast graphics.** Lower thirds or a results tower, always an original design and never a real broadcaster's.

## 5. Sound devices

- **Rhythm from the world.** Lift-chain clicks, start-light beeps, burner blasts, typing on the finger strikes (L). Build the music on the scene's own pulse.
- **Perspective sound.** Everything heard from where the camera is: muffled inside a bag, bursting in when it opens (L: Mac POV).
- **Silence before the hit.** A vacuum gap of 0.1–1 s before the drop or the reveal. The strongest dynamic tool.
- **Tape-stop.** The music winds down to nothing as something sleeps or dies (L).
- **Heartbeat in the dark.** A low pulse as the only sign of life (L: Everest).
- **Nolan ending.** Organ, clock ticks and a rising Shepard tone, a hard cut to black, one tick, then a horn under a wide-tracked title card (L: user favourite, used twice). Rest it for a while now.
- **Genre-true score.** A broadcast sting for sports, a mockumentary cello, a 90s infomercial jingle. The pastiche lives or dies on the sound.

## 6. Endings

- **Product-state shot.** The device showing the product doing its thing: a lid logo breathing, "Done. 3 PRs ready." (L).
- **Nature-made logo.** See the motion signatures above (L: sunrise, city lights).
- **Title card.** Minimal and wide-tracked, with a CTA pointing to the caption (L).
- **Joke caption.** A documentary lower third, a ride photo, a results tower. It carries the brand without a hard sell.
- **Loop back.** The last frame returns to the first.
- **CTA.** Always point to where the link actually is, e.g. "check the caption for the link" when a comment keyword sends it. Hold the end card for about 6 s.

## 7. How to mix

1. Pick the **genre** that best dramatizes the product's verb.
2. Pick a **hook** that makes frame one readable with the sound off.
3. Pick a **structure** the generator can render.
4. Pick **one motion signature**; everything else in the motion layer stays quiet.
5. Pick a **sound idea** that is structural, not just "a beat underneath".
6. Pick an **ending** that pays off the verb and carries the CTA.

Then compare the pick against the last two videos. If three or more shelves match, change the genre first; the other shelves usually follow.
